# Safety Alert Role-Play Script & Submission Guide

**Video Deliverable:** `Week7_Roleplay_YvonneWochuna.mp4` (Duration: ~2–3 minutes)  
**Deliverable Type:** Part B.2 Soft Skills Role-Play  
**HSE Safety Officer:** Yvonne Wochuna  
**Scenario:** Presenting the Week 7 Safety Alert regarding recurring slips/trips at Depot B to a skeptical, seasoned night-shift technician (Dave).

---

## 1. Scenario Context & Learning Objectives

- **The Problem:** Depot B has recorded **33 slip/trip events**—nearly double any other site. Crucially, **82% (27 of 33) occurred on the Night Shift**, specifically around the transfer walkway. Ten of these incidents were classified as high-potential serious injuries (SIFs).
- **The Resistance:** Night-shift technicians often feel singled out, rushed to meet throughput quotas in low-light conditions, and fatigued. They view new safety alerts as management "paperwork exercises" or covert attempts to blame workers for wearing improper gear or moving too fast.
- **The Goal:** Overcome resistance without defensiveness. Use **objective dashboard data** to validate the technician's daily reality, show **empathy** for the physical challenges of night shift, and establish a collaborative partnership focused on protecting people, not assigning blame.

---

## 2. Character Profiles

- **Yvonne Wochuna (HSE Specialist):** Empathetic, calm, grounded in data, collaborative, active listener. Does not recite policy; focuses on getting everyone home safely.
- **Dave (Senior Maintenance Technician, Night Shift):** Hard-working, proud of keeping the depot running, skeptical of corporate initiatives, feels pressured by tight shift schedules.

---

## 3. Dialogue Script (Target Duration: 2 mins 30 secs)

### [0:00 - 0:30] Setting the Scene & The Opening
*(Setting: Break room at Depot B just before the 11:00 PM night shift huddle. Yvonne approaches Dave holding a printed copy of the 1-page Safety Alert.)*

**Yvonne:**  
"Hey Dave, do you have two minutes before the pre-shift huddle kicks off? I wanted to share something with you before we talk to the whole crew."

**Dave:** *(Looks up with a sigh, folding his arms)*  
"Hey Yvonne. Look, if this is about another safety alert or more mandatory checklists, we’re already running two hours behind on the conveyor overhaul. We honestly don't have time to fill out more forms telling us how to walk."

---

### [0:30 - 1:15] Acknowledging Frustration & Introducing Data with Empathy

**Yvonne:** *(Smiles warmly, nods, and sits down beside him)*  
"I completely hear you, Dave. The last thing you or your team need tonight is extra paperwork when you're already pushed to meet maintenance turnarounds. I'm not here to hand you a clipboard or slow you down. I’m here because of what the safety dashboard showed us this morning, and frankly, it worried me."

**Dave:** *(Skeptical)*  
"What did the dashboard show? Someone slipped on a puddle and now safety wants us to wear bubble wrap?"

**Yvonne:**  
"Not quite. Over the last few months, Depot B logged **33 slip and trip incidents**. That’s almost double any other facility in the company. But here’s the critical part: **82% of those slips happened right here on your shift—the night shift—concentrated right around the transfer walkway.** And 10 of those were severe enough that with just an inch of difference, someone could have suffered a spinal injury or broken femur."

---

### [1:15 - 2:00] Handling the Resistance & Collaborative Root-Cause Solving

**Dave:** *(Pauses, uncrosses arms, but remains defensive)*  
"Look, Yvonne... that transfer walkway has bad drainage when the night mist settles, and half the overhead LED fixtures have been flickering for three weeks. If management wants us to stop slipping, maybe fix the lights instead of telling the guys they’re not walking carefully enough!"

**Yvonne:** *(Validating immediately)*  
"Dave, that is exactly why I’m talking to you first. You’re the one walking that floor at 2:00 AM, not the people in the front office. The data told us *where* and *when* people are getting hurt, but you just told me *why*. If that walkway has poor drainage and lighting is cutting out, that’s an engineering failure on our part—it is NOT the crew's fault."

**Dave:** *(Tone softens, surprised)*  
"You really mean that? Because usually when HSE comes down here, it's just: 'Make sure your boots are tied and hold the handrail.'"

**Yvonne:**  
"Holding the handrail matters, but fixing the hazard matters more. Here’s what we’re doing today: Facilities has an emergency work order to replace the walkway lighting before midnight tomorrow. Maintenance is laying down textured high-traction matting on the wet zones this afternoon. What I need from you and the crew right now is just three things: watch your footing during the first and last hour of shift when fatigue peaks, wear the issued slip-resistant boots, and if you spot water pooling, flag it immediately. No write-ups, no blame."

---

### [2:00 - 2:35] Closing & Mutual Commitment

**Dave:** *(Nods approvingly, picking up the alert)*  
"Alright. If facilities is actually fixing the lighting and putting down mats, that’ll make a real difference. I’ll make sure the guys read this during the huddle and remind them to keep their eyes on the walkway."

**Yvonne:**  
"Thank you, Dave. My contact info and direct line are right at the bottom of the sheet. If anything is still unsafe tonight, call me directly, day or night. Let’s get everyone home in one piece."

**Dave:**  
"Fair enough, Yvonne. Thanks for actually listening to us."

*(They shake hands; scene fades out.)*

## 4. Key Rubric Alignment Points Demonstrated

| Rubric Criterion | How This Script Satisfies It |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Persuasion & Empathy (10%)** | Validated the technician's workload stress; agreed that root cause was environmental (lighting/drainage) rather than worker incompetence. |
| **Data-Driven HSE Leadership** | Cited exact dashboard metrics: 33 slips, 82% night shift concentration, 10 potential SIFs, specific transfer walkway location. |
| **Action-Oriented & Jargon-Free** | Clear 3-step technician actions paired with concrete management actions (LED replacement, traction mats). |
